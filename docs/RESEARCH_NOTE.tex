\section{1. Executive Summary}
Low-Level Design (LLD) and Object-Oriented Design (OOD) represent pivotal hiring competencies for Software Engineers across top technology organizations. While Data Structures \& Algorithms (DSA) benefit from mature, automated practice ecosystems (e.g., LeetCode, HackerRank), \textbf{Low-Level Design practice remains profoundly broken}.

\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{The Core Problem:} In DSA, correctness is verified through deterministic input/output test assertions ($f(x) = y$). In Low-Level Design, \textbf{two entirely different architectures for the same problem can both be correct}, depending on scale, concurrency, and operational trade-offs. Without objective, rubric-grounded evaluation and concrete evidence citations, self-practice degenerates into passive diagram memorization or uncalibrated AI hallucinations.
\end{tcolorbox}

This research note analyzes the cognitive failure modes of self-guided LLD practice, benchmarks current market alternatives, identifies three foundational gaps, and articulates a resilient product direction engineered around a focused, iterative feedback loop.

\section{2. The Learner Problem: Why LLD Practice is Broken}
Through systematic investigation of candidate preparation workflows, we identified four interconnected failure stages in the self-practice journey:

\begin{center}
$\text{Ambiguity of Multiple Truths} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{The ``Passive Reading'' Trap} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{The Evaluative Black Hole} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Absence of Delta Feedback}$
\end{center}

\textbf{2.1 The Ambiguity of ``Multiple Valid Truths''} \\
In algorithmic problem-solving, solutions converge on optimal asymptotic bounds ($\mathcal{O}(N \log N)$). Conversely, LLD problems exhibit a divergent solution space: a single-floor parking lot using a synchronized \texttt{List<ParkingSpot>} is optimal for small garages, while an enterprise facility requires an abstract \texttt{ParkingStrategy} (Strategy Pattern), decoupled observer event dispatchers for display boards, and fine-grained lock striping. Without structural evaluation, learners conflate \textit{functional completeness} with \textit{design quality}---assuming that if their pseudocode compiles, their architecture is sound, blind to latent God classes or leaky abstractions.

\textbf{2.2 The ``Passive Reading'' Illusion} \\
Because active evaluation tools are scarce, candidates disproportionately consume passive media: YouTube walkthroughs, blog posts, and static GitHub repositories. Reviewing an expert's finalized UML diagram gives an illusion of mastery. However, in an unprompted interview setting, the learner struggles to decompose business requirements into cohesive domain entities, identify boundary invariants, and decouple interfaces.

\textbf{2.3 The Evaluative Black Hole} \\
Upon drafting a design on paper or in an IDE, the candidate faces unanswered questions that self-study cannot resolve:
(1)~\textit{Did I violate SRP by combining parking fee calculation with ticket persistence?}
(2)~\textit{Is \texttt{Vehicle} tightly coupled to concrete spot allocations?}
(3)~\textit{Will this class hierarchy survive when EV charging stations are introduced tomorrow?}
(4)~\textit{Did I introduce an Abstract Factory unnecessarily where a simple factory was sufficient?}

\textbf{2.4 Absence of an Iterative Delta Loop} \\
Mastery requires feedback followed by immediate re-application. In current practice, candidates rarely revise an attempted problem because they lack an objective metric to verify whether \textbf{Attempt 2 ($A_2$)} successfully addressed the structural flaws of \textbf{Attempt 1 ($A_1$)}.

\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{Key Finding on LLMs:} Unconstrained LLM prompts generate deceptive feedback. Because models are trained for conversational agreeableness, they routinely praise poorly coupled designs. To be pedagogically rigorous, machine evaluation must be constrained to a \textbf{deterministic, evidence-backed rubric} requiring explicit citation of candidate classes and methods.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{4. Key Gaps \& Product Direction: The LLD Practice Studio}
Our audit revealed three foundational unmet needs: \textbf{(1) Multi-Dimensional Rubrics} (dissecting designs across SRP, Cohesion, Coupling, Extensibility), \textbf{(2) Grounded Evidence Citations} (citing concrete classes/methods rather than vague advice), and \textbf{(3) Versioned Attempt Tracking} ($\Delta \text{Score} = \text{Score}(A_{t+1}) - \text{Score}(A_t)$).

The \textbf{LLD Practice Studio} solves this through five foundational architectural principles:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Curated, Constraint-Rich Scenarios:} 5 representative problems (Multi-Floor Parking Lot, Multi-Car Elevator Dispatcher, Splitwise Expense Sharing, In-Memory Cache with Pluggable Eviction, Distributed API Rate Limiter) with explicit operational invariants.
  \item \textbf{Structured Submission Model:} Replaces unstructured freeform text with typed domain inputs: Core Entities (SRP), Interfaces \& Contracts (DIP/OCP), Design Patterns with Justifications, and Concurrency Trade-offs.
  \item \textbf{Active Visual \& Implementation Bridge:} (a)~\textit{Live UML Class Diagram}: Dynamically synthesizes declared classes and interfaces into real-time interactive visual class cards and Mermaid.js diagrams. (b)~\textit{Polyglot Code Exporter}: Translates abstract design specifications into strongly typed, compilable boilerplate in \textbf{Java 17+}, \textbf{TypeScript}, and \textbf{C++20}.
  \item \textbf{Two-Stage Hybrid Evaluation Engine:} Stage 1 (Deterministic) executes sub-50ms checks verifying structural completeness, mandatory entity counts, and interface contracts. Stage 2 (Rubric-Grounded Semantic) scores orthogonal dimensions citing \textbf{Concrete Evidence}, \textbf{Identified Concerns}, and \textbf{Actionable Suggestions}.
  \item \textbf{Stateful Attempt Lifecycle \& Delta Tracking:} Submissions transition through an explicit finite state machine ($\text{DRAFT} \rightarrow \text{SUBMITTED} \rightarrow \text{EVALUATING} \rightarrow \text{COMPLETED}$), guaranteeing zero data loss, idempotency, and mathematical score progression comparison.
\end{enumerate}

\section{5. Summary of Expected Learning Outcomes}
By shifting LLD practice from passive observation to structured active articulation, learners achieve three measurable transformations:
\textbf{(1)~Elimination of God Classes:} Explicit single responsibility fields force learners to decouple coordinators from business logic.
\textbf{(2)~Defensive Concurrency Modeling:} Requiring explicit trade-off explanations compels candidates to address race conditions before writing code.
\textbf{(3)~Calibrated Interview Readiness:} Objective rubric citations mirror senior engineering hiring bars, equipping candidates with explainable design vocabulary.
